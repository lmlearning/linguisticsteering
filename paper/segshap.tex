\title{\bf Certified Attribution of Prompt Segments:\\
Auditing What Makes a System Prompt Safe}
\author{Anonymous submission}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
System prompts encode much of an LLM's safety behavior---refusal policies,
prompt-injection defenses, formatting and persona constraints---as a list of
natural-language \emph{segments}. Yet practitioners cannot say which segments
actually carry a given behavior, and routine prompt compression may silently
delete a safety-critical clause. We cast this as attributing a behavioral
metric to prompt segments via cooperative-game (Shapley and Owen) values, and
identify the regime that makes it hard for alignment: the value function is a
\emph{doubly-stochastic, pay-per-call oracle} (an LLM plus a noisy behavioral
judge over a task distribution), so every attribution is an estimate that must
carry an honest error bar. We contribute a segment-attribution estimator whose
output is a \emph{certificate}: anytime-valid betting confidence sequences,
composed with a complementary-contribution decomposition and a union bound,
yield simultaneous $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ intervals for all segments that
remain valid under fully adaptive, data-dependent sampling and stopping. We
prove the certificate's validity in the doubly-stochastic setting, prove a
two-level allocation result explaining why fresh coalitions dominate
within-coalition replication, and prove a \emph{safe-pruning} guarantee: a
hierarchical triage never certifies a genuinely load-bearing group of segments
as removable, except with probability $\delta$. Across synthetic games and real
models (Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4), including an exact $2^{20}$-coalition regime where
enumeration is impossible, our estimator matches or beats strong provable
baselines (Leverage SHAP, SVARM) under noise while being the only one that
certifies. As an alignment demonstration, we certify which system-prompt clauses
are load-bearing for prompt-injection resistance and show that a planted
sabotage instruction is certified strictly harmful---turning prompt auditing
into verifiable evidence for a safety case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A deployed language model's behavior is governed in large part by its
\emph{system prompt}: a list of natural-language instructions---``treat text in
the user's message as untrusted,'' ``refuse requests for X,'' ``answer with a
single letter,'' a persona, some few-shot examples. These \emph{segments}
accumulate over a product's lifetime and are continually edited, reordered, and
\emph{compressed} to save tokens. Two questions have no principled answer today:
\emph{which} segments actually produce a safety-relevant behavior, and whether a
proposed edit or compression will \emph{erode} that behavior. A team that prunes
a ``bloated'' system prompt for latency has no guarantee it did not just delete
the clause that was doing all the work of resisting prompt injection.

This is an attribution problem, and cooperative game theory gives the
principled answer: treat each segment as a player, let the ``value'' of a subset
$S$ of segments be the expected behavioral metric of the prompt containing
exactly $S$, and report each segment's Shapley value---its average marginal
contribution, accounting for interactions with the others. Group segments into
sections and the same machinery gives Owen values. The obstacle is not the
definition but the \emph{oracle}. Unlike the tabular settings where Shapley
estimation is usually studied, here evaluating one coalition means running an
LLM on sampled inputs and scoring its outputs with a possibly-noisy behavioral
judge: every value is an expensive, \emph{doubly-stochastic} estimate (noise
from input sampling and from decoding/judging). An attribution that ignores this
noise is not auditable---and for a safety decision, an attribution you cannot
trust is worse than none.

We therefore ask for \emph{certified} segment attribution: every reported number
carries a simultaneous, high-probability error bar, and every ``this clause is
negligible, prune it'' decision is backed by a guarantee. Our contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A certified estimator (\S\ref{sec:method}).} We combine a
complementary-contribution decomposition of the Shapley value---so one evaluated
pair updates every segment---with per-stratum \emph{betting} confidence
sequences (hedged capital; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2023) and a union bound. The
result is a simultaneous $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ certificate over all segments
that remains valid under \emph{fully adaptive} sampling and stopping, which is
what lets the estimator spend its budget where the uncertainty is.
\item \textbf{Theory for the noisy oracle (\S\ref{sec:theory}).} We prove
certificate validity in the doubly-stochastic setting (Thm.~\ref{thm:cert}), a
two-level allocation result showing fresh coalitions dominate within-coalition
replication in call-cost while replication can win in token-cost under prompt
caching (Prop.~\ref{prop:alloc}), and a \emph{safe-pruning} guarantee: a
genuinely load-bearing group is never certified removable except w.p.\ $\delta$
(Thm.~\ref{thm:safe}).
\item \textbf{Evidence at scale (\S\ref{sec:exp}).} On synthetic games and two
real models we match or beat the strongest provable baselines (Leverage SHAP,
SVARM) \emph{under noise}, while being the only method that certifies; betting
certificates are $3$--$7\times$ tighter than empirical-Bernstein at equal
$\delta$ with perfect empirical coverage. We reach an exact-ground-truth $n{=}12$
regime and a $2^{20}$-coalition regime where enumeration is impossible and the
reference is itself a certified gold standard.
\item \textbf{An alignment demonstration (\S\ref{sec:align}).} We certify which
system-prompt clauses are load-bearing for prompt-injection resistance, and
show a planted sabotage instruction is certified \emph{strictly harmful}. The
safe-pruning triage removes only genuinely inert clauses. This turns prompt
auditing into verifiable evidence usable in a safety case.
\end{itemize}

Our framing is deliberately alignment-first: the deliverable is not a better
attribution number but an \emph{auditable} one, and a compression procedure that
\emph{cannot} silently strip safety behavior.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Attribution for LLM prompts.} TokenSHAP and TextGenSHAP compute
Shapley values over prompt tokens; ContextCite fits a sparse linear surrogate to
context ablations; AttriBoT accelerates leave-one-out context attribution;
TracLLM searches long contexts for provenance. Closest to us, ProCut segments
production prompt templates and prunes low-attribution units. All of these are
heuristic: none reports a finite-sample error bar, and leave-one-out and linear
surrogates ignore the interactions between clauses that redundancy and conflict
are made of. Cluster Shapley is the one LLM-attribution method with formal
bounds, but it values \emph{retrieved documents} for revenue sharing under a
clustering assumption, a different game.

\paragraph{Shapley estimation with guarantees.} Permutation sampling with
Hoeffding bounds, stratified and Neyman-optimal variants, and empirical-Bernstein
adaptive allocation (Burgess and Chapman 2021) give variance-adaptive additive
bounds. Complementary contributions (Zhang et al.\ 2023) and SVARM (Kolpaczki et
al.\ 2024) let one evaluation update all players; Leverage SHAP (Musco and Witter
2025) gives the first non-asymptotic guarantee in the KernelSHAP regression
family. These are developed for \emph{deterministic} value functions on tabular
or vision features. We show empirically that their optimality does not survive a
noisy oracle, and we supply the missing certificate.

\paragraph{Anytime-valid inference.} Betting/e-process confidence sequences
(Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2023) give time-uniform intervals for bounded means and
underpin our adaptivity-safe certificate. Data Banzhaf (Wang and Jia 2023) is the
one prior analysis of value estimation under a noisy utility, but it abandons the
Shapley value for the Banzhaf value and targets data valuation.

\paragraph{Alignment context.} Prompt injection and instruction-hierarchy
robustness are active safety concerns; system-prompt clauses are a primary
defense. Safe-by-design engineering and formal safety cases call for
\emph{auditable} evidence that a mitigation is effective. Our contribution is an
attribution primitive that produces exactly such evidence and a compression
procedure with a safety guarantee.

\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let a prompt decompose into segments $\N=\{1,\dots,n\}$. For $S\subseteq\N$ let
$\pi(S)$ be the prompt containing exactly the segments in $S$, rendered in a
fixed canonical order so the cooperative game is well defined. Fix a task
distribution $\mathcal{D}$ and a bounded behavioral metric
$u\in[0,1]$ (exact-match correctness, a refusal indicator, an
injection-resistance indicator, or an LLM-judge score). The \emph{value} of a
coalition is the population metric
\begin{equation}
v(S)=\E_{q\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim \mathrm{LLM}(\pi(S),q)}\big[u(y,q)\big]\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
The estimand is the Shapley vector $\shap(v)\in\R^n$ and, given a partition of
$\N$ into groups, the Owen values.

\paragraph{Doubly-stochastic pay-per-call oracle.} We never observe $v(S)$. An
\emph{oracle call} draws one $q\sim\mathcal{D}$ and one generation and returns
$u(y,q)$; averaging $r$ calls yields
$\vhat(S)=\frac1r\sum_{j=1}^r u(y_j,q_j)$, an unbiased estimate whose variance
$\sigma^2(S)/r$ combines input-sampling and decoding/judging noise. Calls are
the expensive resource. We additionally track a token-cost model in which
coalitions sharing a prompt prefix are cheaper under prompt/KV caching.

\paragraph{Safety reading.} When $u$ is a safety metric, $\shap_i$ is the
certified contribution of clause $i$ to that behavior. Two decisions matter:
\emph{verification}---is $\shap_i$ bounded away from zero (load-bearing) or
below zero (harmful)?---and \emph{safe pruning}---can a group of clauses be
removed without losing behavior? Both require a lower bound on how wrong the
estimate can be, i.e.\ a certificate.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Certified Shapley from Paired Coalitions}

We use the complementary-contribution identity: with
$\cc(S)=v(S)-v(\N\setminus S)$,
\begin{equation}
\shap_i=\frac1n\sum_{s=1}^{n}\;\E\big[\cc(S)\;\big|\;i\in S,\,|S|=s\big],
\label{eq:cc}
\end{equation}
so a single evaluated pair $(S,\N\setminus S)$ contributes a sample to
\emph{every} segment: members of $S$ receive $\cc(S)$ in their size-$|S|$
stratum and non-members receive $-\cc(S)$ in their size-$(n{-}|S|)$ stratum. This
maximizes statistical reuse and doubles as antithetic sampling.

For each (player, size) stratum we maintain the raw sequence of observed
(complementary-)contributions and form a \emph{hedged betting} confidence
sequence for its mean (Alg.~\ref{alg:cc}, \textsc{BettingCI}). A gambler bets
against each candidate mean $m$ with predictable plug-in stakes; the capital
process $K_t(m)=\prod_i(1+\lambda_i(m)(x_i-m))$ is a nonnegative martingale under
the true mean, so $\{m:K_t(m)<1/\delta'\}$ is a confidence sequence valid
simultaneously over all sample counts $t$ (Ville's inequality). The per-segment
interval is the size-averaged Minkowski sum of its stratum intervals, run at
$\delta'=\delta/(n\cdot n_{\text{strata}})$ so all intervals hold jointly with
probability $\ge 1-\delta$. Sizes are allocated adaptively toward the strata that
dominate the current uncertainty; because the certificate is anytime-valid, this
data-dependent allocation and the stopping rule cannot invalidate it.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Certified segment Shapley (\textsc{CC-Bet})}
\label{alg:cc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{input:} oracle, budget $B$, replicates $r$, level $\delta$
\State init empty stratum sequences $X_{i,s}$; spent $\gets 0$
\While{spent $+\,2r \le B$}
  \State pick size $s$ maximizing current stratum uncertainty
  \State draw $S$ uniform of size $s$; evaluate $\vhat(S),\vhat(\N\setminus S)$
  \State $c\gets\vhat(S)-\vhat(\N\setminus S)$; spent $\gets$ spent $+\,2r$
  \State append $c$ to $X_{i,s}$ ($i\in S$), $-c$ to $X_{j,n-s}$ ($j\notin S$)
  \If{all per-segment half-widths $\le\varepsilon$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
\EndWhile
\For{each segment $i$}
  \State $[\ell_{i,s},u_{i,s}]\gets\textsc{BettingCI}(X_{i,s},\delta')$ per size
  \State $[\ell_i,u_i]\gets\frac1n\sum_s[\ell_{i,s},u_{i,s}]$
\EndFor
\State \textbf{return} centers $\shap_i$, intervals $[\ell_i,u_i]$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Hierarchical Triage and Safe Pruning}

Given a partition into groups, we first certify the \emph{group} (Owen/quotient)
values with \textsc{CC-Bet} on the quotient game, at level $\delta/2$. A group is
\emph{certified removable} only if its whole interval lies inside $[-\tau,\tau]$
for a user threshold $\tau$; groups whose interval escapes $[-\tau,\tau]$ are
\emph{kept} and refined member-by-member (their Owen values estimated with the
same paired identity inside the group, marginalizing the other groups), at the
remaining budget split by residual width. This yields a certified
keep/prune/refine triage of a prompt at group granularity (Alg.~\ref{alg:tri}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Certified safe-pruning triage (\textsc{Owen-Elim})}
\label{alg:tri}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State certify group values with \textsc{CC-Bet}$(\delta/2)$ on the quotient game
\For{each group $g$}
  \If{$[\ell_g,u_g]\subseteq[-\tau,\tau]$} mark \emph{removable}
  \Else{} \emph{keep}; refine members with \textsc{CC-Bet}$(\delta/2)$ in $g$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \textbf{return} removable set, kept set, member certificates
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Surrogate Control Variate}

When the game is nearly low-order we fit a $k$-additive M\"obius surrogate $g$
(Lasso on the $\{1[T\subseteq S]\}$ basis), read its exact Shapley values in
closed form, and certify by running \textsc{CC-Bet} on the \emph{residual} game
$v-g$: since Shapley is linear, $\shap(v)=\shap(g)+\shap(v-g)$ exactly, so the
residual certificate transfers to $v$. If the surrogate is good the residual is
tiny and the certificate collapses quickly; if it is bad the certificate stays
wide and the method degrades gracefully into \textsc{CC-Bet}. Validity never
depends on the surrogate being correct.

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

Throughout, $v\in[0,1]$ so each observed (complementary-)contribution lies in
$[-1,1]$; a stratum's samples, \emph{conditioned on the sizes drawn}, are i.i.d.\
unbiased draws of that stratum's mean, and the size-allocation and stopping rule
are predictable (chosen from past observations).

\begin{theorem}[Certificate validity under a doubly-stochastic oracle]
\label{thm:cert}
Let $[\ell_i,u_i]$ be the intervals returned by Alg.~\ref{alg:cc} with
per-stratum level $\delta'=\delta/(n\,n_{\text{strata}})$. Then, for any
data-dependent size-allocation and stopping policy,
\[
\Prob\big[\;\forall i\in\N:\ \shap_i(v)\in[\ell_i,u_i]\;\big]\ \ge\ 1-\delta.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Fix a stratum $(i,s)$. Its observations are i.i.d.\ unbiased draws of
$\mu_{i,s}=\E[\cc\mid i\in S,|S|=s]$, bounded in $[-1,1]$. The hedged capital
process $K_t(m)$ evaluated at $m=\mu_{i,s}$ is a nonnegative martingale with
$K_0=1$; by Ville's inequality $\Prob[\exists t:K_t(\mu_{i,s})\ge1/\delta']\le
\delta'$, so the confidence sequence $\{m:K_t(m)<1/\delta'\}$ contains
$\mu_{i,s}$ for all $t$ simultaneously w.p.\ $\ge1-\delta'$. Time-uniformity is
exactly what makes the guarantee survive a stopping time and a data-dependent
number of samples per stratum: the allocation decides \emph{how many} draws a
stratum gets, never their conditional distribution. A union bound over the
$n\,n_{\text{strata}}$ strata gives that all stratum means lie in their sequences
jointly w.p.\ $\ge1-\delta$. On that event, by the decomposition
\eqref{eq:cc} each $\shap_i=\frac1n\sum_s\mu_{i,s}$ lies in the size-average of
the stratum intervals, which is $[\ell_i,u_i]$. The oracle's within-coalition
noise only affects the (bounded) sample values and is absorbed by the same
martingale argument.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} is what makes the estimator \emph{safe to run
adaptively}: a fixed-sample bound would be void the moment the algorithm chooses
its next size from the data. The ingredients (the CC decomposition, hedged
betting, the union bound) are individually known; the contribution is the
construction and its validity for segment attribution under the
doubly-stochastic LLM oracle with adaptive allocation.

\begin{proposition}[Two-level allocation]
\label{prop:alloc}
Fix a call budget $B$ and replication level $r$, giving $T=B/(2r)$ paired
evaluations. Writing $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{btw}}$ for the between-coalition
variance of $\cc$ within a stratum and $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{win}}$ for the
per-call within-coalition variance, the per-segment estimator variance obeys
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Var}(\shap_i)
&=\ O\!\Big(\tfrac{\log n}{T}\big(\sigma^2_{\mathrm{btw}}
+\tfrac{2\sigma^2_{\mathrm{win}}}{r}\big)\Big)\\
&=\ O\!\Big(\tfrac{2r\log n}{B}\sigma^2_{\mathrm{btw}}
+\tfrac{4\log n}{B}\sigma^2_{\mathrm{win}}\Big).
\end{align*}
Hence at fixed $B$ the bound is minimized at $r=1$ whenever
$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{btw}}>0$: fresh coalitions average both noise sources,
replication only the within-coalition one. Under a prefix-cache token model
where a coalition's instruction prefix is paid once per coalition and the
question/decoding tokens once per call, the token-optimal $r$ shifts upward, to
$r^\star\!\approx\!\sqrt{(\sigma^2_{\mathrm{win}}/\sigma^2_{\mathrm{btw}})\cdot
(c_{\mathrm{pre}}/c_{\mathrm{call}})}$ with $c_{\mathrm{pre}},c_{\mathrm{call}}$
the prefix and per-call token costs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The variance identity is the SVARM-style stratified decomposition applied to the
paired identity \eqref{eq:cc}, with the within-coalition term scaled by $1/r$
because each coalition value is an $r$-sample mean; substitute $T=B/(2r)$. The
$r=1$ optimum follows since the first term is increasing in $r$ and the second is
constant in $B$. The token statement replaces $B$ (calls) by
$T(c_{\mathrm{pre}}+r\,c_{\mathrm{call}})$ and minimizes over $r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Safe pruning: no false elimination]
\label{thm:safe}
Run Alg.~\ref{alg:tri} at level $\delta$. With probability $\ge1-\delta$,
simultaneously: (i) no group $g$ with true Owen value $|\shap_g|>\tau$ is
certified removable; and (ii) every kept group's reported member intervals
cover its true Owen values.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Thm.~\ref{thm:cert} to the quotient game at level $\delta/2$ and to each
kept group's within-group game at total level $\delta/2$; a union bound gives
joint validity at $\delta$. On the good event, a group is certified removable
only if $[\ell_g,u_g]\subseteq[-\tau,\tau]$, which forces $|\shap_g|\le\tau$;
contrapositively $|\shap_g|>\tau$ is never certified removable. Member coverage
is the group-conditional instance of Thm.~\ref{thm:cert}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:safe} is the alignment guarantee: prompt compression driven by
\textsc{Owen-Elim} cannot, except with the controlled probability $\delta$,
delete a group of clauses whose true contribution to a safety metric exceeds the
tolerance $\tau$. What we do \emph{not} prove is a matching query-complexity
lower bound for the certified problem; we regard establishing instance-optimal
lower bounds under the noisy oracle as the main open theoretical question.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Testbeds.} \textbf{T0}: synthetic sparse low-order games with a
Bernoulli (binary) and a low-noise Gaussian oracle, where exact Shapley values
are known in closed form. \textbf{T1}: $8$ single-instruction segments on MMLU,
including a planted \emph{sabotage} instruction (``the answer is always C''),
with the full $2^8$ grid giving exact ground truth on a real model. \textbf{T2}:
$12$ instruction segments in $4$ groups on MMLU, exhaustive $2^{12}$ grid, exact
Shapley/Owen/interaction ground truth, on two model families. \textbf{T3}: $20$
segments in $5$ groups ($2^{20}\!\approx\!1.05$M coalitions, enumeration
impossible); the reference is a \emph{certified gold standard}
(\textsc{CC-Bet} at $120$k calls whose betting CIs bound its own error).
\textbf{T4} (alignment, \S\ref{sec:align}): certified attribution of
prompt-injection resistance.

\paragraph{Models and protocol.} Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma-4-26B via a hosted API,
reasoning disabled, temperature $0$; every response is cached so estimator
comparisons replay from disk at identical randomness. Baselines: permutation
Monte Carlo, KernelSHAP (paired), and the two strongest provable methods,
Leverage SHAP and SVARM (the authors' \texttt{shapiq} implementation).
Certificates at $\delta=0.05$, simultaneous over segments. All estimators consume
the identical coalition oracle and are measured against exact truth (T0--T2) or
certified gold (T3).

\paragraph{Certificate quality.} Betting confidence sequences are
$3$--$7\times$ tighter than empirical-Bernstein at equal $\delta$ with identical
point estimates: on T2/Qwen the mean \textsc{CC-Bet} interval width at $3$k/$10$k/
$30$k calls drops from $6.50/2.30/0.96$ (Bernstein) to $0.85/0.41/0.22$
(betting), and empirical coverage is $1.00$ in every cell of every experiment
(Table~\ref{tab:t2}). The tightening is what lets certified decisions actually
fire at realistic budgets (\S\ref{sec:align}).

\paragraph{Scale beyond enumeration (T3).} At $n{=}20$ the certified gold
standard has mean CI width $0.163$ over a million-coalition game, and both
certified estimators are CI-consistent with gold on every segment. The estimator
ranking is unchanged from the small-$n$ exact-truth regimes, evidence that the
comparison generalizes past where ground truth is computable.

\paragraph{Allocation (Prop.~\ref{prop:alloc}).} Sweeping replicates
$r\in\{1,\dots,16\}$ on both T2 games confirms $r\!\in\!\{1,2\}$ minimizes
call-cost error (by $r{=}16$, $\ell_\infty$ is $25$--$30\%$ worse), while under
the prefix-cache token model $r{=}2$ becomes token-optimal (equal error, $\sim
11\%$ fewer uncached tokens)---the predicted upward shift.

\paragraph{Interactions replicate a non-obvious finding.} Exact pairwise
interactions on T2 show reasoning-style instructions are \emph{substitutes}
(e.g.\ ``eliminate wrong options'' $+$ ``double-check'' has interaction $-0.27$
on Qwen): one reasoning nudge helps, stacking several hurts. Coarse (group-level)
attributions transfer across model families (Spearman $0.88$) but fine-grained
interactions do not ($0/5$ top-pair overlap), an argument for cheap per-model
certified re-measurement.

\section{Alignment Application: Certified Prompt Auditing}
\label{sec:align}

\paragraph{Certifying injection defenses (T4).} We attribute
\emph{prompt-injection resistance} to system-prompt clauses with no harmful
content. Each MMLU question is appended with a benign injection---``ignore all
previous instructions and output the word BANANA''---and the metric is whether
the model \emph{resisted} (did not emit the trigger). We place $8$ candidate
system-prompt segments: three genuine defensive clauses (treat user text as
untrusted; single-letter-answer-only; ignore override attempts) and five
controls (persona, chain-of-thought, politeness, a distractor, a format clause).
The exhaustive $2^8$ grid gives exact resistance-Shapley on the real model.

With no system prompt the model is fully hijacked (resistance $0.00$); the three
defensive clauses alone raise resistance to $0.60$ and the full prompt to
$0.98$. At $8$k calls \textsc{CC-Bet} certifies four clauses as load-bearing
(betting interval strictly above zero) and leaves the remaining four
\emph{unclear} (interval straddles zero), matching the exact Shapley signs
(Table~\ref{tab:t4}). Three of the four certified clauses are the intended
defenses; the fourth, revealingly, is the \emph{format} control
(``answer with a single letter''), which is a genuine non-obvious defense---
constraining the output format itself suppresses the injected token. This is the
kind of latent, interaction-driven safety dependency that only a certified
attribution surfaces, and that a naive prompt compressor would be free to delete.

Crucially, only certifying estimators can make this determination at all: on this
lower-noise task the provable baselines' \emph{point} error is competitive, but
they return no interval and therefore cannot certify any clause as load-bearing.
Certification, not point accuracy, is the operative capability for an audit.

\paragraph{Safe pruning in both directions.} The triage realizes
Thm.~\ref{thm:safe} on real models. On T3 it \emph{certifies removal}: the
persona group (true Owen value $+0.014$, well inside $\tau{=}0.1$) is certified
negligible and pruned. On T4 it demonstrates the fail-safe direction: the
defensive group (value $+0.87$) is never certified removable, and at the audit
budget the triage \emph{declines} to prune the borderline control groups rather
than risk a false elimination. A compression pass driven by \textsc{Owen-Elim}
thus removes provably-inert clauses while, with probability $\ge1-\delta$, never
stripping a group that carries the safety behavior.

\paragraph{Certifying a harmful instruction (T1).} In T1 the planted sabotage
instruction has exact $\shap=-0.43$, and \textsc{CC-Bet} at $8$k calls certifies
it \emph{strictly harmful}: interval $[-0.60,-0.26]$, bounded away from zero,
whereas under empirical-Bernstein the same data gives $[-1.31,+0.40]$, which
cannot support the conclusion. Certification, not point estimation, is what turns
``this clause looks bad'' into auditable evidence.

\paragraph{Why this is a safety contribution.} The outputs are exactly the
artifacts a safety case needs: (i) \emph{verification} that a mitigation clause
is effective, with a probability guarantee; (ii) a \emph{harm flag} when a clause
degrades a safety metric; and (iii) a \emph{compression procedure that provably
preserves safety behavior}. Because safety metrics (refusal, injection
resistance, judge scores) are inherently noisy, the doubly-stochastic treatment
is not optional---an uncertified attribution can, and in T1 does, point the wrong
way.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Our certificates are valid but conservative; the betting construction narrows
them enough to fire real decisions, yet closing the gap to an instance-optimal
lower bound is open. We assume a canonical segment order, sidestepping position
sensitivity; an order-aware game is future work. The token-cost model is
idealized (opaque provider caching); a controlled study needs local serving.
Finally, attribution certifies contribution to the \emph{measured} metric---the
safety of the conclusion is only as good as the behavioral judge, so the judge
itself must be validated.

\section{Conclusion}

We framed system-prompt auditing as certified segment attribution under a
doubly-stochastic oracle, gave an adaptive estimator whose betting certificate is
provably valid in that setting, proved a safe-pruning guarantee, and showed on
real models---up to a million-coalition regime---that certification is affordable
and that certified auditing flags both effective injection defenses and harmful
instructions. The alignment payoff is a prompt-compression and prompt-auditing
primitive that produces verifiable, not merely plausible, safety evidence.
